# Composition moves

A move is a decision a senior designer makes about the whole page or one section. Pick moves, then build components inside them. Every move names tier-A references in the design-refs library; open them before using the move (`refs.image(id)`), and measure one live (`refs.measure(url)`) when type or spacing matters.

## First-screen moves

Choose exactly one. The first screen is a thesis, not a header.

**Object as hero.** One product or object, photographed or rendered at full scale on a quiet ground; headline small above or below it.
Works when the product is physical or has one iconic form. Fails when the object is generic (a laptop, a phone with a UI) or the photo is stock.
Refs: `apple-com`, `apple-com-macbook-air`, `silosilosilo-com`, `vaonis-com-pages-product-atlas`, `stereoscopecoffee-com`, `teenage-engineering`.

**Type as hero.** The headline or wordmark is the image: viewport-wide, tight tracking, one committed face.
Works for brands with a strong name or a short, sharp claim. Fails with long headlines, a timid face, or when something else on the screen competes.
Refs: `shopify-design`, `casagay-com`, `springsummer-dk`, `kopetzki-law`, `area-tech`, `postevand-com`.

**Owned graphic.** One authored shape or illustration system nobody else has: a ribbon, a folded band, light streaks, a character world.
Works when it can recur across the page and brand. Fails when it is a generic blob, mesh gradient or 3D glass icon.
Refs: `stripe-com`, `increase-com`, `raycast-com`, `family-co`, `posthog-com`.

**Documentary photograph.** A real scene from the customer's life, not a product shot: a cluttered desk, a shop street, a hand raising the product to the sky.
Works for human, warm or trust-heavy products. Fails with stock smiles, posed teams, or photos that could belong to any company.
Refs: `claude-com`, `faire-com`, `postevand-com`, `heartaerospace-com`, `etoile-com`.

**Surreal single image.** One impossible but calm photograph that states the promise: a desk on a mountain.
Works for software that is otherwise invisible. Fails if it needs explaining or if it is AI-image glossy.
Refs: `mercury-com`, `topology-vc`.

**Manifesto.** A paragraph of real prose at display size, with no hero visual competing.
Works for companies with a point of view (research, policy, studios). Fails when the prose is marketing filler.
Refs: `anthropic-com`, `manifesto-endel-io`, `springsummer-dk`.

**Near-empty.** A mark, one sentence, a few links. Confidence by withholding.
Works for known brands and holding pages. Fails for unknown products that must explain themselves fast.
Refs: `thebrowser-company`, `block-xyz`, `vercel-com`.

**The product at work.** The real interface, large and legible, doing one specific job, with a short headline. Not a tilted mockup, not a grid of floating fragments.
Works for tools whose UI is the selling point. Fails when the UI is generic or shrunk to illegible.
Refs: `linear-app`, `ramp-com`, `superpower-com`, `amie-so`.

**The world as interface.** The whole site behaves like the product's metaphor: a desktop OS, an archive, a configurator.
Works when the metaphor is true to the product. Fails as decoration.
Refs: `posthog-com`, `archivio-garonzi1925-com`, `findmy-vitra-com-en-en-questionnaire-chairs`.

## Section moves

**Index or list instead of cards.** Services, features or products set as a typographic list, an index with numbers, or columns of text.
Refs: `kopetzki-law`, `portorocha-com`, `archivio-garonzi1925-com`.

**Spec sheet.** Product facts typeset like hardware documentation: labels, values, rules, tiny caps.
Refs: `teenage-engineering`, `apple-com-iphone`.

**Lineup strip.** The whole range in one horizontal row of small items on white, working as navigation.
Refs: `becaneparis-com`, `apple-com-iphone`.

**Full-bleed interruption.** A single edge-to-edge photograph or color field between dense sections to reset the eye.
Refs: `heartaerospace-com`, `cloudflare-com`, `glossier-com`.

**Live proof.** A real counter, document or data point rendered as itself: GDP processed, an invoice, a schedule.
Refs: `stripe-com`, `ramp-com`.

**Editorial gallery.** Large object photos with serif captions and prices set like a catalogue.
Refs: `1stdibs-com`, `fidele-editions-com`.

**News as columns.** Updates set like a newspaper: dates, headlines, short text in columns.
Refs: `portorocha-com`, `ibm-com`.

**Ticker of titles.** A slow horizontal run of names or titles in a display face.
Refs: `press-stripe-com`.

**A real close.** The page ends on a decisive statement or action with space around it, not a fourth CTA band and a mega footer.
Refs: `apple-com`, `claude-com`.

## Type moves

- **One face with character, used at extremes.** Huge display, small text, little in between. Refs: `shopify-design`, `springsummer-dk`.
- **Serif display on a technical product.** Signals judgment and editorial voice. Refs: `claude-com`, `anthropic-com`, `resend-com`.
- **Condensed caps over imagery.** Industrial confidence. Refs: `anduril-com`, `bellroy-com`.
- **Italic as accent, not decoration.** A few words or titles. Refs: `press-stripe-com`, `spotify-com-us-premium`, `thebrowser-company`.
- **Tiny mono or caps labels with lots of air.** Only where the content is technical or archival. Refs: `teenage-engineering`, `nextjs-org-conf`.

## Color moves

- **Drenched field.** One brand color owns a whole region. Refs: `cloudflare-com`, `glossier-com`.
- **Restrained ground plus one strong accent.** Refs: `linear-app`, `vercel-com`, `ramp-com`.
- **Color from photography.** The palette comes from the photographs. Refs: `heartaerospace-com`, `faire-com`, `etoile-com`.

## Using moves

1. Pick one first-screen move, four to six section moves, one type move and one color move.
2. Say for each why it fits this product's truth, not just why it looks good.
3. Never use a move only because a reference used it; take the decision, not the layout.
4. If two candidate moves both fit, pick the one the category uses less.
